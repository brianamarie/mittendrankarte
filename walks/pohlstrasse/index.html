Walking the Pohlstraße

Before the doors

Three things come back

The name of the street. This street has had three. The one on the sign is Ottilie Pohl. For a long time nothing here said who she was. Other names come later, written at a door. Some articles say the street’s name once, and then talk about something else.

What is left. After the war, eleven houses stood out of nearly a hundred. On the way you pass a school, a football ground, a path, and a window. Some of them are gone.

Who uses the street now. Neighbours. Children, for an afternoon. A kindergarten. Rooms under the railway arches. A bar. People arguing about who may plant the pavement, or about a gate that shuts.

The name: Paul Enck, „Straßen im Kiez: Pohlstraße“, 23 October 2020. What was left: J. Lückerath, „Zwischen 1970 und 2025: Bilder aus der Pohlstraße“, 11 February 2026.

Pin · the name of the street

Three names, then a new numbering

From 10 June 1862 until 7 March 1936 this was Steglitzer Straße. The postcard still carries that name: neobaroque fronts of about 1870, running west toward the bend at Potsdamer Straße.

On 7 March 1936 it became Ludendorffstraße. On 31 July 1947 the city renamed it Pohlstraße, after Ottilie Pohl. She never lived here.

In 1936 the house numbers changed too. The old numbers ran up one side and back down the other. The new ones alternate: even on the right, odd on the left, from Dennewitzstraße. Kauperts, and one article, date the name from 1946. The ordinance is 31 July 1947.

The postcard is from the collection of Ralf Schmiedecke, printed in „Noch einmal: Wo war die Körner Realschule?“, 14 March 2026. Street dates: Luisenstadt, Steglitzer Straße. Verordnungsblatt für Groß-Berlin, 1947, page 218. Paul Enck, „Straßen im Kiez: Pohlstraße“, 23 October 2020.

The name on the sign

Ottilie Pohl lived in Moabit, and was killed in Theresienstadt.

Born Taube Levit on 14 November 1867 in Schönwalde, near Luckau, she signed her name Taube. A milliner, she came to Berlin and in 1893 married Wilhelm Hermann Pohl, a coachman who later worked in a factory. Three children. One son died as a baby.

When the ban on socialist organizations ended in 1890, she joined the Social Democrats. In the First World War she opposed the war and moved to the Independent Social Democrats. She and her son handed out the Spartakus letters. In 1920 Tiergarten elected her to the Berlin city council, to the committees for poor relief and for schools. Later she joined the Communists.

After 1933 she and Rosa Lindemann worked in the Rote Hilfe, looking after the children of people under arrest. Her last home was Beusselstraße 43, in Moabit.

The Gestapo arrested her in 1940, at seventy-three. On 20 November 1942 she was deported to Theresienstadt. She died there in December 1943, at seventy-six.

The records disagree about the prison. One account takes her from the women’s prison on Barnimstraße. The other gives eight months on Kantstraße, release at the end of 1941, and deportation the next year because she was Jewish. Both agree on the camp and the month.

On 31 July 1947 Berlin took Ludendorff’s name off this street and put hers on it. The stone in the photograph is not on this street. It is in the pavement at Beusselstraße 43, laid 10 May 2025, and it names the Kantstraße prison and 2 December 1943. A porcelain plaque hung on that house from 1989 and was gone by 2018. Paul Enck walked this street in 2020 and found nothing that said who she was. A Berlin memorial register records a supplementary name-sign here since 2021.

Pin 44 · and the house at number 2

Eleven houses left, and one last demolition

Joseph Lückerath moved into a shop flat at Pohlstraße 2, corner of Flottwellstraße, in April 1970: 67.78 marks for 55 square meters. The house had been a hotel for railway workers. In 1978 he was given notice, and a flat at number 44. Number 2 was demolished soon after, the last house taken for a feeder to the Westtangente, a motorway still on the plans, and the last home on Flottwellstraße. A hotel stands on the corner again. His later photograph is on the right. The camera has moved. The corner has not.

At the end of the war, he writes, eleven houses were left of nearly a hundred. From number 44 he saw the backs of the houses on Kurfürstenstraße: the Pohlstraße plots held only one-story sheds. A new row went up between 1984 and 1986. Numbers 1, 3, and 5, at Dennewitzstraße, waited until 2012.

J. Lückerath, „Zwischen 1970 und 2025: Bilder aus der Pohlstraße“, 11 February 2026. All four photographs are his.

Pin · Pohlstraße 11

Thirteen arches, a youth club since 1998

About twenty-five years before the article of September 2022, the neighborhood association took on the thirteen railway arches at number 11. Artists and makers have worked there since 2001. Pins · numbers 16, 18 and 20

The school was behind the houses

The Körner secondary school opened on 13 October 1887 as the III. Höhere Bürgerschule, three classes and 145 boys. Temporary rooms first: Kurfürstenstraße 144, then Derfflingerstraße 18a. In autumn 1891 it moved to its own building at Steglitzer Straße 8a: twelve classrooms, a physics room, a drawing room, an assembly hall, a gym, and flats for the director and the caretaker. Named in 1912 for the poet Theodor Körner. On the 1915 postcard the gate still reads „III. Höhere Bürgerschule“, between the old numbers 8 and 9.

The building is gone from the street and still on the map. Histomap, set at today’s number 16, lays the 1910 plan over the street: that overlay is the photograph. The building file says the classrooms survived the war and were later storage and workshops. The old address books say 8a, which after renumbering should be door 16. The articles also put the passage behind today’s 20, the old numbers 9 and 10. Three markers, one school.

„Die Körner-Realschule“, 10 December 2024. „Wo war die Körner-Realschule?“, 18 January 2025. „Noch einmal: Wo war die Körner Realschule?“, 14 March 2026.

The street pins · the Spielstraße

The road belongs to the children for an afternoon

A Spielstraße is a play street: for a few afternoons the road is closed to cars and given to children. On this map it fills the middle of the street. The neighborhood fund paid for the materials. Drivers were slow to get used to it. The photograph is one of those afternoons.

The argument that lasted was the cars. On the European car-free day in 2025 the public-order office said it could not tow cars off a play street, because a play street was not a public road. The police wrote tickets. In April 2026 the office agreed to tow.

The street pins · the pavements

Twelve hundred square meters, then an argument about who may plant

In March 2024 the roads office began lifting the pavement on Pohlstraße and Kluckstraße, between Potsdamer Straße and Lützowstraße, and planting underneath. Twelve hundred square meters. Tree pits widened and joined. Sidewalks brought to at least two and a half meters. Almut Neumann announced it. Where a café had a forecourt, paving stayed so the tables could sit between the beds. The photograph is one of those beds.

Residents were told not to plant. Some beds were already green, and people on Kluckstraße collected signatures. The FDP asked the borough assembly to shrink the unpaved area. The neighborhood forum answered, unanimously, that the beds should stay: they water the trees. The motion failed.

The street pins · a walk with the police

Cellar break-ins, and no quick remedy

Neighbors reported break-ins in cellars, trespass, disturbances, drug use and prostitution in doorways, and more tents. Houses and cellars on Pohlstraße had been broken into several times in half a year. The photograph is neighbors and officers in a courtyard off the street.

A count on Flottwellstraße found 340 cars an hour. More than 300 an hour counts as a burden. Laura Fritsche told fifty neighbors that 87 percent of journeys here are on foot, by bicycle, or by public transport, and that 20 percent of residents own a car. Pohlstraße is one edge of the traffic-calming area between Schöneberger Ufer, Potsdamer Straße, Kurfürstenstraße, and the park.

Pins · the street, and number 58

Möbel Hübner, from a joinery to a closing sale

The day after Christmas 2025 the car park was full. The signs said everything must go, thirty percent off. That car park is the photograph. Berlin knew the shop by „Ich soll Sie schön grüßen“. Friedrich Wilhelm Hübner, a master joiner born around 1850 in Schwerz, came to Berlin about 1880 with his wife Helene and, with the carpenter W. Schenck, opened a joinery and furniture shop at Lützowstraße 14. A 1908 photograph shows the window at Steglitzer Straße 27.

The present owners, the house of Türklitz, believed they descended from Jewish cloth merchants in Brandenburg an der Havel, baptized around 1650 as Tuchelitz. Enck read the handwritten indexes in the cathedral archive. In 1639 a Jewish man was baptized at St. Katharinen and named Christianus; his former name was Jacob, and no family name was written, because Jews then had none in that register. The local family book has one Jacob Tüchelmann made a citizen in 1624. The descent cannot be shown.

„Möbel Hübner schließt nach 115 Jahren“, 27 December 2025, photo FP. „Geschichte von Handel und Gewerbe (7): Möbel Hübner“, 29 February 2024.

Pin · Pohlstraße 60

Gunter Demnig lays two stones for the Schnirlins

On 8 October 2022 Gunter Demnig laid the stones for Ossip and Gertrud Schnirlin at number 60.

Ossip Schnirlin, born 3 March 1868 in Proskuriv, now Ukraine, to Judko Schnirlin and Chane, born Feige, studied with Joseph Joachim and played as a soloist. A finger injury turned him to teaching and to editing violin music. On 7 April 1906, in Berlin, he and Max Reger gave the first performance of Reger’s Suite im alten Stil. A second article calls him the last musician to play with Reger: a trio in Jena on Easter Monday, seventeen days before Reger died in a Leipzig hotel, at forty-three.

Gertrud was a concert pianist and a musicologist. The Nazi race laws made no exception for either of them. The photograph is private, taken shortly before their deaths.

On 9 November that year the SPD of Tiergarten Süd and the same initiative walked Lützowstraße, Potsdamer Straße, and Pohlstraße, the anniversary of the pogrom. The custom is to clean all 69 Stolpersteine in Tiergarten Süd on 9 November and on 8 May.

Pins · number 62, and a table at number 75

Fourteen girls, and the school behind a wall

Planned with the Falk boys’ school, the Charlottenschule was a public high school for girls in a wealthy district with fewer public schools than most. To spare the private schools, which lived on fees, only six classes opened at Easter 1880, the top two at Michaelmas. The public school still charged 25 marks a quarter, 100 marks a year. The plot ran from the boys’ school at Lützowstraße 83 to Steglitzer Straße 29, today’s Pohlstraße 62, with a wall between them. You entered from Steglitzer Straße.

Leopold Engel and G. Wenzel, an apple-wine shop at number 29, offered the land in 1870. Engel wanted 78,000 marks, and listed the rents of seven households in number 28, about 3,000 talers a year.

The photograph, fourteen girls of about sixteen in studio dress around 1900, changed hands across a table at Les Climats. Roland Kretschmer introduced Enck to Cheryce von Xylander and to Bubar, who cooks the crêpes. A new book’s essay follows the school and one pupil, Gertrud Bruck, born 1875, who married the manufacturer Hans Siegfried Cramer. Hermann Muthesius designed their Dahlem house, Pacelliallee 18–20, now used by Stanford University. The Cramers left for America in 1933. The table was number 75. The school is number 62.

„Die Charlottenschule in der Steglitzer Straße“, 27 October 2024. „Die Backfische der Charlottenschule“, 5 September 2026. Photograph of the pupils, photographer unknown, printed with permission.

Pin · Pohlstraße 75

Les Climats, and a dance named for the street

Roland Kretschmer runs the wine bar at number 75. The house’s own page says the shop has been there since 2008, and the bar since 2011. The wine is Burgundy, bought from the growers. On 6 September 2024 the invitation said Les Climats was turning sixteen, and the birthday was a Pohlonaise.

The Pohlonaise is the summer evening on the pavement. The invitations, as mitteNdran prints them, start at 18:00: crêpes and galettes from Bubar’s truck, wine and crémant, and a band. On 12 May 2023 they described the round: a glass while you wait at the truck, the band, children playing, then dancing, then back to the truck. The Hot Club of Berlin plays jazz manouche inside through the colder months, two guitars and a bass, every other week. They call the Pohlonaise the open-air version: a crêpe truck, sparkling wine, and a quartet outside. The invitation of 26 June 2024 names that Friday’s band: Vero Nouk singing, George Birkett on guitar, Martin Buhl on double bass, Leo Gertsner on drums. The play-street meetings happen here because this is where the neighbors already are. The house page lists Tuesday to Thursday from 16:00 to 23:00, Friday and Saturday from 14:00 to midnight, and Sunday and Monday closed.

It is a Friday, at 18:00, through the terrace season. In 2024 the first was 3 May, and the bar called that the opening. The last that year was 20 September, to close the summer. In 2023 the last was 6 October, before the evening got too cold. From 2 October 2024 the jam sessions were indoors again, every other Wednesday.

Pin · Pohlstraße 64

Hedwig Kubatzky had her studio at number 64

On 24 June 2024 ten stones for the Kubatzky family were laid across Berlin. Three, in this quarter, went into the pavement at Lützowstraße 87, now the Allegro schoolyard: Hermann Julius, Rosa, born Arndt, and their son Herbert, who fled to Palestine in the 1930s and survived. The photograph is those stones, not the door on this street.

The same day the guests walked to Pohlstraße 64. Hedwig Kubatzky, born 13 January 1875 in Zippnow, Pomerania, was a seamstress and a milliner in Berlin. Until 1940 her studio was at Ludendorffstraße 64, this house. She had to give the flat up. Her last address was a Judenhaus, where Jews were forced to live, at Turmstraße 9. Deported to Riga on 27 November 1941, she was shot with the others who survived the journey, in the Rumbula forest on 30 November.

Her sister Johana, born 1873, was deported from the Gipsstraße collection camp on 15 August 1942 and murdered in Riga as well. No Berlin address was found, so her stone is here, beside Hedwig’s.

Oliver Staack bought Wanda Kubatzky’s postcards to her fiancé at a flea market, traced the family from Deutsch Krone, and in the pandemic found Debbie Kirby through a genealogy site. Theo Bröcker, of the memorial-stone initiative in Mitte, spoke for the two sisters.

Pin · Pohlstraße 89

The house that was occupied, and the Verein that came out of it

In 2025 the Stadtteilverein Tiergarten turned forty. The association is less known than its center. Pohlstraße 89, the house it calls its cradle, was to be demolished for the Südtangente, a motorway planned roughly parallel to this street, from Lietzenburger Straße across Möbel Hübner’s old car park to a junction where Gleisdreieck park is now. Several young people occupied number 89 and kept it standing. The council of people affected by the plan became the association, and opened a neighborhood-management office beside the wall at Potsdamer Platz. New houses on the empty eastern plots, old houses repaired, and in 2006 the center, which the association still runs. The photograph is the anniversary walk, paused in a courtyard.

The demolition at number 2 and this occupation are the two ends of one plan. Beside them sits a broadcast line that draws a motorway from the Urania. These articles are the ones that name the houses.

The same pins · articles that only touch the street

One sentence of Pohlstraße, and a subject somewhere else

A large share of the 109 articles mention the street only once. They belong on the walk, and they are about something else.

Werner Herzog, in the photograph, cast Bruno S. in Jeder für sich und Gott gegen alle (1974). Old neighbors remember him with a plastic bag and an accordion, singing in the street. Herzog then wrote Stroszek for him. The German Wikipedia says the script took five days, after the part in Woyzeck went to Klaus Kinski. Bruno plays a street singer just out of prison. That is the end of this street

You are at the west end. The street has had three names: Steglitzer Straße, Ludendorffstraße, Pohlstraße. Ottilie Pohl lived in Moabit, and she was killed in Theresienstadt. Her memorial stone is at Beusselstraße 43. On this street her name is the sign. The articles are the sentence under it.
